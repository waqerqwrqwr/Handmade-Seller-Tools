5 Pricing Mistakes Small Handmade Sellers Make

Five common problems to check before publishing a handmade listing.

Pricing errors often look harmless on one product. Repeated across dozens of orders, they can quietly reduce the money your business keeps. Here are five mistakes worth checking.

1. Pricing from materials alone

Materials are visible, so they are easy to count. Labor and selling costs are less obvious, but they still affect the economics of the product.

2. Treating your labor as free

If a product takes an hour to make, that hour has an opportunity cost. Even a simple internal hourly rate can make your pricing decisions more realistic.

3. Forgetting selling fees

Marketplace and payment fees can take a meaningful share of a sale. They should be part of the pricing calculation, not an afterthought.

4. Using competitor prices as your formula

Competitor prices can provide market context, but they do not tell you your own costs. Two sellers can offer similar products while having very different labor, sourcing, and overhead structures.

5. Looking at revenue instead of profit

A strong-looking sales number does not automatically mean a strong business. Track the money left after relevant costs and compare products on a consistent basis.

A simple pre-listing check

  • Do I know my complete per-unit cost?
  • Did I include my labor?
  • Did I account for selling fees?
  • Is the target profit deliberate?
  • Can I repeat the calculation for the next product?
