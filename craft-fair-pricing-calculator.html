Use different units-sold scenarios before booking an event. A price that looks profitable at 60 sales can behave very differently if the market produces only 25 or 30 sales.

Start with the per-item contribution: selling price minus direct unit cost and percentage payment fees. Then compare that contribution with the fixed cost of the event and the number of items you expect to sell.

A craft fair price has to cover more than the item itself. Fixed event costs such as the booth, travel, setup, and other event expenses reduce what you actually keep from sales.

How to Price Products for a Craft Fair

Craft fair costs worth checking

  • Booth or event fees
  • Product cost per item
  • Card or payment processing fees
  • Travel, setup, and other fixed event costs

How this calculator works

Event profit = (selling price − direct unit cost − payment fee) × units sold − fixed event costs

Example

At $360 fixed costs, $9 direct cost, $30 selling price, 3% payment fees, and 60 units sold, estimated event profit is about $474.

Important assumptions

Results are planning estimates. Replace the default assumptions with your actual costs, rates, shipping arrangements, discounts, and sales volume. Marketplace and payment fees can change over time.

How do I know if a craft fair is worth it?

Estimate the contribution you earn from each sale, subtract the event’s fixed costs, and test realistic sales volumes. The number of units you actually sell matters as much as the price.

Should craft fair prices be different from online prices?

They can be. In-person selling may have different fees, shipping costs, and customer expectations. Compare the total cost structure of each sales channel before setting the final price.

Can I use this for my own business?

Yes. Enter your own numbers to create a simple planning estimate. The calculators are designed for comparison and everyday decisions.
