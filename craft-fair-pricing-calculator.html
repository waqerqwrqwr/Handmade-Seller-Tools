REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE

Example: pricing for a weekend craft fair

Suppose booth, travel, helper, and other event costs total $360 and you expect 60 sales. That adds $6 event cost to each item. If direct unit cost is another $9, base cost is $15 before payment fees and target profit. If sales fall to 30 units, event cost per item doubles to $12.

Practical tip: Use your own costs and fee assumptions before making a pricing decision.

HOW IT WORKS

Price the event, not just the product

A craft fair has economics that an online listing does not: booth cost, travel, setup time, and uncertain sales volume. Include those fixed costs before deciding whether a price works.

Recover the event cost

Booth fees and travel are fixed event costs that need to be recovered across your expected sales.

Estimate realistic volume

Your break-even point depends heavily on how many units you actually sell.

Test the target-profit price

The calculator can work backward from a desired event profit.

FORMULA

Understand the calculation

Knowing the formula makes it easier to adjust the tool to your own business model.

Event profit = selling price × units × (1 − payment fee%) − unit cost × units − event fixed costs Break-even units = event fixed costs ÷ [selling price − unit cost − selling price × payment fee%]

FAQ

Common pricing questions

Should I include my time at the craft fair?

You can treat your event time as another fixed cost or use a target profit that compensates you for the time.

How many units should I expect to sell?

Use a conservative estimate based on your past events, audience, event size, and product price range.

Should booth cost be spread across products?

Yes. This calculator handles it as a fixed event cost and recovers it through total event sales.

Does the target-profit price guarantee I will make that profit?

No. It shows the price required under your expected-unit assumption. Actual results depend on sales volume and other costs.
