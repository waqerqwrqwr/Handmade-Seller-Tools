HOW TO CHOOSE

How to choose the right calculator

Match the tool to the decision in front of you

Each group above answers a different kind of question, and most sellers move between them in a fairly predictable order. Treat this section as a map rather than a checklist. You rarely need every tool at once, but knowing which one applies saves time and keeps you from guessing.

Start with the decision, not the tool

If you are setting a price for the first time, start with the pricing calculators. If you already have a price and want to know what you keep, start with the fee and profit tools. If you are planning how much to make, or whether an event is worth attending, start with break-even and event planning. Working backward from your question keeps the numbers meaningful instead of abstract.

Which group suits which seller

The Etsy and marketplace tools suit anyone selling on a fee-charging platform, where a small change in percentage rates moves your margin more than people expect. The resale and secondhand tool suits sellers who list the same kind of item in more than one place and want to compare net payouts side by side. The handmade and craft tools suit makers whose costs include their own labor and materials, and who also sell in person at markets and fairs. The profit and business tools are the general-purpose layer, covering margins, markup, break-even, wholesale, and product economics, which applies to almost any small operation.

A common workflow

Many sellers follow the same sequence: estimate your true cost per unit, set a price that covers fees and leaves a margin, check profit at the volume you actually expect to sell, then confirm the result against break-even. You can run that loop with the free calculators on this page, and move the same assumptions into a spreadsheet once the numbers start to repeat. Because every tool uses editable assumptions, you can test a change, whether a higher material cost, a new fee rate, or a discount, without starting over.

None of these tools tell you what to charge. They show what your own numbers imply, so the final decision stays yours.
