```

What Is Etsy Profit?

Etsy profit is the amount of money left after you subtract the costs of making, selling, and fulfilling an order from the revenue generated by that order.

This is different from revenue. Revenue tells you how much money your products generated. Profit tells you how much you actually kept after expenses.

For example, suppose you sell a handmade product for $35. After Etsy fees, materials, labor, packaging, shipping, and other costs, your actual profit could be much lower than $35.

That is why Etsy sellers should calculate profit before deciding whether a product is worth selling.

The Etsy Profit Formula

A simple way to calculate Etsy profit is:

Etsy Profit = Total Revenue − Etsy Fees − Product Costs − Fulfillment Costs − Other Costs

Your exact calculation may vary depending on your business model and the fees that apply to your Etsy shop.

For a more detailed calculation, you can separate your costs into several categories:

  • Product materials
  • Labor
  • Packaging
  • Shipping
  • Etsy listing fees
  • Etsy transaction fees
  • Payment processing fees
  • Advertising costs
  • Currency conversion costs
  • Refund and return reserves
  • Other operating expenses

What Etsy Fees Should You Include?

Etsy has several types of seller fees. The exact fees that apply to your shop can depend on your location, payment setup, advertising activity, and other factors.

Etsy currently identifies listing fees, transaction fees, and payment processing fees as common selling fees. Additional costs can apply for services such as advertising, shipping labels, currency conversion, and certain regulatory fees.

1. Listing Fees

Etsy charges a listing fee when you publish a listing. Etsy currently states that the standard listing fee is $0.20 USD per listing. Listings generally remain active for four months or until they sell, subject to Etsy's listing rules.

If you sell multiple quantities from a listing, additional listing-related charges can apply to additional quantities or renewals.

2. Transaction Fees

Etsy currently charges a 6.5% transaction fee on the total order amount in the designated listing currency. Etsy states that this can include the item price, shipping, and gift wrapping when those charges apply.

3. Payment Processing Fees

Payment processing fees are separate from Etsy's transaction fee. They consist of a percentage plus a fixed amount, and the applicable rate depends on the country associated with the seller's bank account.

4. Offsite Ads

If an order is attributed to Etsy Offsite Ads, an additional advertising fee may apply. Etsy currently states that the rate can be 15% or 12% depending on the seller's sales level, with a per-order maximum of $100.

5. Other Possible Costs

Depending on your business and location, you may also need to consider currency conversion, regulatory operating fees, shipping labels, taxes on certain seller services, or other applicable charges.

Important: Etsy fees can change and may vary by seller location. Always check Etsy's current fee information for your market when setting your pricing.

Don't Forget Your Product Costs

Etsy fees are only one part of your cost structure. A product can have healthy-looking sales but still produce little profit if its production costs are too high.

Materials

Include the actual cost of materials used to make one product. If you buy materials in bulk, calculate the approximate cost used per finished item.

Labor

Labor is often overlooked by handmade sellers. Even if you make the product yourself, your time has economic value.

For example, if a product takes 45 minutes to make, you can estimate your hourly target rate and assign a labor cost to that product.

Packaging

Include boxes, envelopes, labels, tissue paper, stickers, inserts, protective materials, and other packaging expenses.

Shipping

If you offer free shipping, shipping is still a real business expense. If you charge customers for shipping, remember that marketplace fees may apply to the shipping amount as well. Etsy states that its transaction fee applies to shipping charges when you charge buyers for shipping.

Other Costs

Depending on your business, other costs may include advertising, software, photography, design, refunds, damaged products, payment-related expenses, and other operating costs.

Etsy Profit Calculation Example

Let's look at a simplified example.

Selling price $35.00
Materials $8.00
Labor $5.00
Packaging $1.50
Shipping cost $4.00
Other costs $1.00

Before Etsy fees, the product-related costs in this example are:

$8 + $5 + $1.50 + $4 + $1 = $19.50

The seller therefore has $15.50 remaining before Etsy fees and any other applicable costs.

This demonstrates why looking only at the selling price can give a misleading impression of profitability.

How to Calculate Etsy Profit Step by Step

Step 1: Enter Your Selling Price

Start with the price the customer pays for your product.

If you offer discounts, coupons, or sales, consider calculating profit using the actual amount you expect to receive rather than your full advertised price.

Step 2: Add Shipping Revenue

If customers pay for shipping separately, include the shipping amount when calculating the total order revenue and applicable Etsy fees.

Step 3: Estimate Etsy Fees

Account for applicable listing, transaction, payment processing, advertising, currency conversion, and other fees.

Step 4: Calculate Product Costs

Add materials, components, ingredients, blanks, or other direct product costs.

Step 5: Add Labor

Estimate how much labor is required to produce one order. This is especially important for handmade products.

Step 6: Add Packaging and Shipping

Include packaging and the actual cost of fulfilling the order.

Step 7: Calculate Net Profit

Subtract all relevant costs from the revenue generated by the order.

Net Profit = Revenue − All Applicable Costs

Step 8: Calculate Profit Margin

Once you know your profit, calculate your margin:

Profit Margin = Net Profit ÷ Revenue × 100

Profit margin helps you compare different products even when their selling prices are different.

Understanding Etsy Profit Margin

Profit margin tells you what percentage of your revenue remains as profit after your costs.

For example, if your shop generates $100 in revenue and you keep $30 after applicable costs:

$30 ÷ $100 × 100 = 30% Profit Margin

There is no single profit margin that is automatically right for every Etsy seller.

A handmade product requiring several hours of labor may need a different margin than a digital product, print-on-demand product, or simple craft supply.

The important thing is to understand your own costs and make sure your pricing supports the business you are trying to build.

How to Increase Etsy Profit

1. Improve Your Pricing

If your products are consistently selling but producing very little profit, your price may be too low.

Before lowering prices to compete, calculate your actual cost structure.

2. Increase Average Order Value

Bundles, sets, variations, and complementary products can increase the amount a customer spends per order.

3. Reduce Material Waste

Small amounts of unused material can become significant costs when you sell hundreds of products.

4. Track Labor

If you do not track your production time, you may accidentally create products that generate revenue but do not pay you fairly for your time.

5. Review Advertising Costs

Advertising can generate sales, but those sales should be evaluated based on the profit remaining after advertising-related costs.

6. Identify Low-Margin Products

Not every product in your shop needs to have the same margin. But you should know which products generate strong profit and which products consume time without producing enough return.

7. Review Your Numbers Regularly

Product costs, shipping rates, marketplace fees, and advertising expenses can change. Reviewing your pricing and profit calculations regularly helps prevent outdated pricing from slowly reducing your margins.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate Etsy profit?

Start with your total revenue and subtract Etsy fees, product costs, labor, packaging, shipping, advertising, and other applicable business costs. The amount remaining is your estimated profit.

What Etsy fees should I include when calculating profit?

Common costs include listing fees, transaction fees, and payment processing fees. Depending on your situation, you may also need to consider Offsite Ads, Etsy Ads, currency conversion, shipping labels, regulatory operating fees, taxes on certain services, and other applicable charges.

Is Etsy revenue the same as Etsy profit?

No. Revenue is the money generated by sales. Profit is what remains after the applicable costs of selling and fulfilling those sales are deducted.

What is a good Etsy profit margin?

There is no universal ideal margin. Your target should depend on the product, labor involved, material costs, shipping, advertising, returns, and other operating expenses.

Can I calculate Etsy profit before listing a product?

Yes. In fact, calculating expected profit before publishing a listing can help you choose a more sustainable price.

Does Etsy charge fees on shipping?

Some Etsy fees apply to the order amount including shipping. For example, Etsy states that its transaction fee applies to shipping charges when shipping is charged to the buyer. Payment processing fees can also include shipping amounts.

Do Etsy fees vary by country?

Yes. Payment processing rates vary according to the location of the seller's bank account, and additional fees may depend on the seller's location and circumstances.
