REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE

Example: from $12 cost to a selling price

If a product costs $12 and you apply a 75% markup, the markup amount is $9 and the selling price is $21. That $9 profit is a 42.9% margin on the selling price. A 75% markup therefore does not mean a 75% profit margin.

Practical tip: Use your own costs and fee assumptions before making a pricing decision.

HOW IT WORKS

Turn cost into a selling price

Markup is one of the simplest pricing methods: start with cost and add a percentage. The important detail is remembering that markup and margin measure different things.

Markup starts with cost

A 100% markup means adding an amount equal to the cost.

Price is cost plus markup

Selling price = cost + markup amount.

Margin will be lower than markup

Because margin is measured against the larger selling price.

FORMULA

Understand the calculation

Knowing the formula makes it easier to adjust the tool to your own business model.

Markup amount = cost × markup% Selling price = cost × (1 + markup%) Implied margin = markup amount ÷ selling price

FAQ

Common pricing questions

What does a 100% markup mean?

It means the markup amount equals the cost. A $20 cost with 100% markup gives a $40 selling price.

What margin does a 100% markup produce?

A 100% markup produces a 50% margin before other costs.

Should I price handmade products with markup alone?

Markup is a useful starting method, but handmade sellers should also consider labor, overhead, fees, market positioning, and demand.
