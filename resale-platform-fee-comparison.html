Net payout by platform — sorted best to worst
Higher payout is not the only factor: audience size, how quickly items sell, and listing effort matter too.

Selling one item on two platforms can produce two very different payouts, and the difference is rarely obvious from the headline fee. This tool puts the platforms side by side: enter your price, the shipping you charge, and the fee rates that apply to each platform, then rank them by what you actually keep.

Why the same item earns different amounts

Three things move the payout, and they are not the same on every platform:

  • Who pays the fee. On some platforms the seller absorbs the commission. On others the seller is charged little or nothing and a buyer-side charge is added to the buyer's total. A "free to sell" headline describes who is charged, not whether money leaves the transaction.
  • Whether shipping is included. Many platforms calculate their percentage on the whole order — the item price plus the shipping you charge — so raising your shipping figure raises the fee as well.
  • Whether there is a fixed component. A flat per-order charge barely registers on a $120 item and can be the single largest deduction on a $12 one.

Because these three vary independently, the platform with the lowest headline percentage is not automatically the one that pays you most on a given sale.

How to compare platforms fairly

  • Use the same order everywhere. Change one input at a time, or you end up comparing prices rather than fees.
  • Enter the rates that apply to you. The defaults are editable starting points, not official numbers. Real rates depend on your country, your account, the category, and the payment method.
  • Check whether the rate applies to shipping. If it does, include the shipping you charge in the price field so it is part of the calculation.
  • Look at fixed fees separately on cheap items. Compare the ranking at a low price and again at a high one; it often changes.
  • Re-check before you list. Fee structures change, and a comparison built last year may no longer hold.

What the two rate inputs mean

The calculator uses two numbers per platform: a percentage and a fixed amount charged per order.

  • Percentage — the share of the order the platform takes. If several percentage charges apply, enter the combined figure.
  • Fixed fee — the flat amount charged per order, whatever the price.

Where a platform charges the buyer rather than the seller, enter 0 for the seller-side rate. That reflects who pays, not whether a charge exists — the buyer's total is simply higher.

Worked example

Take a $30 item with $5 charged for shipping, so the buyer pays $35, and a $10 item cost. Using the default rates in the tool:

  • Vinted (0%): fees $0.00 → you keep $35.00 → profit $25.00
  • Mercari (10%): fees $3.50 → you keep $31.50 → profit $21.50
  • Depop (10%): fees $3.50 → you keep $31.50 → profit $21.50
  • Etsy (9.5% + $0.45): fees $3.78 → you keep $31.23 → profit $21.23
  • eBay (13.6% + $0.30): fees $5.06 → you keep $29.94 → profit $19.94
  • Poshmark (20%): fees $7.00 → you keep $28.00 → profit $18.00

The spread between the best and worst outcome is $7.00 on a single order — the same item, the same price, a different platform. On ten sales a month that is $70, which is why it is worth comparing before you list rather than after.

The ranking is also price-sensitive. Run the same comparison on a $12 item and the fixed fees take a much larger share of the order, so platforms that add a flat charge move down. Cheap items reward platforms with no fixed fee; expensive items punish high percentages.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing headline percentages. A platform that charges the buyer can look free to sellers while still shaping the buyer's total. Compare the payout, not the marketing.
  • Forgetting that the percentage hits shipping. If you charge $8 for shipping and the platform takes 13%, more than a dollar disappears before you have paid the courier.
  • Using rates from memory. Fees differ by country, category and account level, and they change. Look them up or ask the platform.
  • Leaving the fixed fee at zero. On low-priced items it is often the biggest single deduction.
  • Assuming the cheapest platform is best. Payout is one factor. Audience size, how fast items sell, and the effort of listing all affect what a platform is actually worth to you.

How to use the result

  • Run the comparison at two or three price points before deciding where to list a whole category of items.
  • If two platforms are close, weigh the audience and the time to sell, not just the cents.
  • Re-run the numbers whenever a platform announces a fee change, and keep the rate inputs up to date.

Important assumptions

The defaults are editable assumptions, not official platform rates. Fee structures differ by country, category, account type and payment method, and several platforms have moved charges between the buyer and the seller in recent years. The model does not include promoted listings, currency conversion, return costs, taxes, or subscription fees. Confirm the current rates on each platform's own fee page and enter them yourself before you make a decision.

Do I keep more on Vinted or Depop?

It depends on the rates that apply to your account and your country, and on whether the fee is charged to you or to the buyer. Enter both sets of rates here and compare the payout — the tool does not assume a winner.

Why do some platforms start at 0%?

Because some platforms charge the buyer rather than the seller. Enter 0 for the seller-side rate to reflect that, or replace it with whatever rate you actually pay.

Does the percentage apply to shipping?

On many platforms the percentage is calculated on the whole order, including the shipping you charge. If that is how your platform works, include shipping in the price field so the fee is calculated correctly.

Why include a fixed fee?

Some platforms add a flat per-order charge on top of the percentage. It is small in absolute terms but hits low-priced items hardest, so it can change which platform pays most.

Can I use this outside the United States?

Yes. Every rate is editable, so enter the numbers for your own country. Only the defaults are US-oriented starting points.

Are these official platform rates?

No. Simple Tool Works is an independent calculator site and is not affiliated with any marketplace. Always confirm current fees with the platform itself.
